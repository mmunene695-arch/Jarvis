# Interview Cheat Sheet — read this the night before

## The 60-second answer to "Tell me about yourself"

Not your life story. Three beats: **now → how you got here → why here.**

> "I'm currently [role] at [place], where I [one achievement with a number]. I got into this
> field after [one-line origin]. I'm here because [one specific thing about this organisation],
> and it matches the work I want to do more of."

Practise it out loud until it takes 60 seconds. Not 3 minutes.

---

## The STAR method — use it for every "Tell me about a time…"

- **S**ituation — one sentence of context
- **T**ask — what you had to do
- **A**ction — what *you* did (say "I", not "we")
- **R**esult — **always a number**

Prepare 6 STAR stories beforehand. They cover almost any question:

1. A time you solved a difficult problem
2. A time you dealt with a difficult customer or colleague
3. A time you made a mistake (be honest — own it, then say what you changed)
4. A time you worked under pressure / to a deadline
5. A time you showed leadership (leading does not require a job title)
6. A time you improved something (a process, a number, a system)

---

## Questions you should ask them (ask at least 2)

- "What does success look like in this role after 90 days?"
- "What is the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How does the team measure performance?"
- "What happened to the last person in this role?"

Do **not** ask about salary in a first interview unless they raise it. If pushed: *"I'm open to discussion based on the full package — what range have you budgeted for the role?"*

---

## Kenyan-specific things that come up

- **"Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"** — They are testing commitment, not prophecy. Give something concrete and plausible: *"Leading a branch team / running my own unit here."*
- **Salary expectations.** Know the market rate for the role before you walk in. Google it, ask two people in the field. Never say "anything."
- **Church / tribe / hometown questions** are inappropriate and rare in formal settings. Deflect politely: *"I'd prefer to keep it professional, if that's alright."*
- **The "can you start immediately?" test** — always yes, unless you are genuinely contracted elsewhere.
- **Bring:** original certificates + copies, National ID, KRA PIN certificate, and 3 printed CVs. Even for online interviews, have the folder ready in one place.

## The 10 questions with a hidden agenda

| They ask | They really want to know |
|---|---|
| "Why should we hire you?" | Can you connect your work to our problem? |
| "What is your weakness?" | Are you self-aware and improving? (Name a real one + what you do about it.) |
| "Why did you leave your last job?" | Are you a flight risk or a complainer? (Never insult a former employer.) |
| "Tell me about a conflict." | Do you escalate everything, or can you resolve things? |
| "How do you handle pressure?" | Will you fold on a bad day? |
| "Where else are you interviewing?" | How fast do we need to move? (Be honest, don't name names.) |
| "What are your salary expectations?" | Are you in budget? |
| "Do you have questions?" | Did you prepare? (Always have two.) |
| "Describe your ideal manager." | Are you difficult to manage? |
| "Any reason you couldn't relocate/travel?" | Practical constraints |

---

## After the interview (the step almost everyone skips)

Within **2 hours**, send:

> Dear [Name], thank you for the conversation today. I particularly enjoyed discussing [one
> specific thing]. It confirmed my interest in the role — I'm keen on [one thing you'd do].
> Looking forward to your feedback. — [Your name]

Short, specific, no grovelling. It genuinely moves decisions.

Then: note down every question they asked you, while you still remember. That is your study material for the next one.
