## Mistake 1 — Your CV is being read by software, not a person

Most Kenyan banks, NGOs, telcos and large employers use an **ATS** (Applicant Tracking System). It is software that scans your CV for keywords before a human sees it.

**What kills you:** tables, text boxes, columns, headers/footers, logos, and graphics. The software cannot read them and drops your content silently.

**Fix it:**
- Plain, single-column layout. No tables. No text boxes.
- Put your name in the body text at the top, **not** in a header.
- Use standard headings: `SUMMARY`, `EXPERIENCE`, `EDUCATION`, `SKILLS`.
- Save and send as **.docx** unless they specifically ask for PDF.

**Test:** copy the text of your CV and paste it into a plain text editor. If it comes out as gibberish, the software sees gibberish too.

## Mistake 2 — You wrote duties, not results

**Weak:**
> Responsible for customer service and handling customer complaints.

**Strong:**
> Handled 40–60 customer queries daily at a Nyeri retail outlet; reduced complaint escalations by 30% over six months by introducing a simple follow-up log.

The rule: **verb + what you did + number + outcome.** If you genuinely have no numbers, use scope — how many customers, how many people, how much money, how often.

Action verbs to steal: *led, built, cut, grew, trained, negotiated, processed, resolved, coordinated, launched, saved, automated.*

## Mistake 3 — Your summary says nothing

Delete this line: *"A hardworking, dedicated and results-oriented individual seeking a challenging position."*

Every employer has read it 10,000 times. It contains zero information.

Replace it with **who you are + what you can do + proof**, in two lines:

> Customer service officer with 3 years in busy Nyeri retail, handling 50+ customers daily and reconciling daily cash of KES 80,000. Trained 4 new cashiers. Fluent in English, Kiswahili and Kikuyu.

---

## Mistake 4 — You listed every job, including the irrelevant ones

Nobody needs your 2017 attachment details in 8 lines. Depth on the last 10 years, one line on anything older.

Order matters: **most recent first**, always. Never reverse-chronological-by-mistake — recruiters do notice.

---

## Mistake 5 — No keywords from the job advert

The ATS scores you on how closely your CV matches the advert's language. If they ask for *"cash reconciliation"* and you wrote *"counting money"*, you score zero.

**Fix it:** before applying, copy the job advert. Highlight every skill word. Make sure the exact same words appear in your CV, naturally, in your real experience. Do this every single time. It is the single highest-leverage 10 minutes in a job search.

## Mistake 6 — Contact details that fail

- Use a **professional** email: `firstname.lastname@gmail.com`. Not `cutie254@`, not `nyeriboy@`.
- One phone number, in the format `+254 7XX XXX XXX` — international format so diaspora and NGO recruiters can reach you.
- Add your town: *"Nyeri, Kenya (willing to relocate)"* — it removes a doubt before it forms.
- Add your LinkedIn **only if** it actually looks professional.

---

## Mistake 7 — You sent the same cover letter to everyone

A cover letter is not a summary of your CV. It answers one question: **why you, for this job, at this organisation.**

Three paragraphs is enough:
1. The role + one specific reason you want *this* organisation (name something real about them).
2. The one or two achievements that make you obviously qualified.
3. What you will do in the first 90 days + a clean close.

If it takes more than 15 minutes, it is too long.

## The 10-minute checklist before you hit send

- [ ] One page (two only if you have 10+ years)
- [ ] Single column, no tables, no graphics
- [ ] Name and phone in the body, not a header
- [ ] Summary is specific, with a number in it
- [ ] Every bullet starts with an action verb
- [ ] At least 5 keywords from the advert appear verbatim
- [ ] Dates are consistent and reverse-chronological
- [ ] Saved as `.docx`, filename: `Firstname-Lastname-CV.docx` (not `CV final final(2).docx`)
- [ ] Read aloud once — typos survive spellcheck, not your mouth
- [ ] Someone else read it
